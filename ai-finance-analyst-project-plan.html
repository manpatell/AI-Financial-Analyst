Project plan · v1 · Nov 2026

Ledgerline: an AI finance analyst that finds money and never makes up a number

An agent that reads a company's invoices, bank transactions and e-invoices, then reconciles them, catches duplicate payments and payment fraud, forecasts cash, and answers finance questions. Every number links back to its source document. (Ledgerline is a working name. Check it's free before you launch.)

1. What the research says

Why this is worth building now, and where the gaps are.

0.8–2%

of all disbursements are duplicate or wrong payments, per APQC benchmarks. Even top performers sit around 0.8%.

$3.05B

in business email compromise losses reported to the FBI in 2025. A classic version is the fake "our bank details changed" email.

62%

of mid-market finance executives have struggled to manage or scale cash flow forecasting (PYMNTS, Aug 2026).

1 Sep 2026

From this date, every VAT-registered business in France must be able to receive structured e-invoices (Factur-X, UBL, CII). SMEs must start issuing them in Sept 2027.

$1B

Rillet's valuation after its $100M Series C in Aug 2026. Investors are betting heavily on AI-native finance tools.

86–91%

Typical field accuracy of GPT-4o-class LLMs on invoice extraction in public benchmarks. Line items and tables are the weak spot.

What the big players already do

  • Invoice capture, GL coding and approval routing (Vic.ai, Tipalti, Stampli)
  • Close and reconciliation (BlackLine, Rillet)
  • Automatic transaction categorisation; Puzzle claims up to 98%

The gaps you can show off

  • Trust: finance teams fear made-up numbers. Separate approaches report the best results when the LLM decides the steps and plain code does the maths.
  • Silos: AP, reconciliation and forecasting usually live in separate tools. Shared data is where the biggest gains are.
  • Fuzzy duplicates: most ERPs only catch exact matches, so "INV-5521" and "5521-OPS" both get paid.
  • France e-invoicing: it's new and messy, and few portfolio projects handle Factur-X.

2. The pitch in one sentence

"Upload a year of a company's invoices and bank statements. In three minutes Ledgerline finds €18,400 in duplicate payments, blocks one fake bank-change request, reconciles 96% of transactions, and tells you the runway is 14 months. Click any number to see the exact invoice it came from."

That's your demo video. Each feature below exists to make one part of that sentence true and provable.

3. Features that add real value and that you can show off

Coreneeded to work · Valuesaves money or time · Flexsets you apart technically

1
FlexValue

"Proof for every number": the LLM can't do maths

The LLM only plans the steps and picks tools. All arithmetic, filters and joins run as SQL or Python. Every figure in an answer or report carries a provenance ID you can click to open the invoice line or bank transaction behind it. A validator blocks any answer containing a number that doesn't trace to a tool output.

The #1 fear of finance teams. You can say "0 unverified numbers across 500 eval questions" and back it up.

2
FlexCore

E-invoice native: Factur-X, UBL and CII first, PDF as fallback

When the structured XML is present (from Factur-X PDFs or UBL files), the parser uses it and gets the fields 100% right. Scanned or plain PDFs go through vision-LLM extraction with per-field confidence scores. A panel shows the share of invoices arriving structured versus scanned.

Any Paris recruiter or CFO knows about the Sept 2026 reform. Very few portfolio projects handle it.

3
ValueFlex

Fuzzy duplicate payment hunter

Cleans up invoice numbers, vendor names and amounts, then scores invoice pairs with fuzzy matching plus a small classifier. It catches the patterns ERPs miss: changed references ("INV-5521" vs "5521-OPS"), the same invoice arriving through two channels, credit notes never applied, and the same vendor saved under two names. Output: "€X recoverable", with each pair shown side by side.

Turns straight into money saved, the strongest headline for a demo.

4
Value

Vendor master clean-up (entity resolution)

Merges "Acme Industrial", "Acme Industrial Inc" and "ACME IND" into one vendor using VAT and SIREN numbers, IBAN, address and name similarity. Merges you approve feed back into the matching rules.

Duplicate detection is impossible on dirty vendor data. Fixing this first is what a senior engineer would do.

5
ValueFlex

Bank-change fraud guard

Flags any invoice whose IBAN differs from the vendor's payment history, a new vendor paid unusually fast, or an urgent "updated bank details" email. Risk-scores each one, holds the payment, and generates a callback checklist using the phone number on file, never the one in the email.

Payment redirection is a multi-billion-dollar problem every year. "Blocked a fraud attempt" makes a great demo moment.

6
CoreValue

Reconciliation agent that works by exception

Matches bank transactions to invoices one-to-one, one-to-many (a single payment for several invoices) and many-to-one (partial payments). Rules come first, then ML scoring, then the LLM only for leftover cases such as unclear payment references. Humans only see the exceptions.

The month-end close is the job that keeps finance teams late. Show "96% auto-matched, 14 exceptions to review".

7
ValueFlex

Cash forecast with honest uncertainty, plus what-if scenarios

A 13-week cash forecast from known invoice due dates, recurring costs and learned customer payment delays, with conformal prediction intervals (an 80% band that actually holds 80% of the time). Runway in months. Scenarios in plain language, such as "hire 3 engineers in March" or "the biggest client pays 30 days late", applied as structured changes to the model.

Cash forecasting is the CFO's top priority. Backtested intervals show statistical maturity, not just a nice chart.

8
FlexCore

Governed autonomy: a policy file controls what the agent may do

A readable policy.yaml sets the limits, for example: auto-approve matches above 0.95 confidence under €1,000; anything over €5,000 or with a changed IBAN needs a human. Every decision is logged with model version, input lineage, confidence, the rule that applied, who approved it and when. Human corrections feed back into the thresholds.

This is the 2026 enterprise conversation ("governed autonomy" and audit trails). It shows you think like someone shipping to real finance teams.

9
Flex

Synthetic company simulator with planted problems

Generates a realistic French SME: about 40 vendors, 2,000 invoices, 12 months of bank activity, seasonal patterns. It then plants known problems: 25 duplicates (exact and fuzzy), 3 fraud attempts, vendor price creep, unused subscriptions, late payers. The answer key lets you score every feature. Open-source it.

This solves the "no real data" problem and becomes a reusable benchmark others can cite. Few portfolio projects ship their own evaluation environment.

10
Flex

Public eval report and leaderboard

Extraction accuracy per field and per line item on SROIE, CORD and FATURA plus your own messy French invoices. Agent task success on 500 finance questions. Detection precision and recall on the simulator. Cost and latency per document for each model. Published as a page in the repo.

Shows you evaluate, not just build. Engineering managers notice this first.

11
FlexValue

Cost-smart model routing

Clean structured invoices skip the LLM entirely. Easy PDFs go to a small, cheap model, and only low-confidence ones go to a frontier model. A dashboard shows € per 1,000 documents and accuracy for each route.

Real teams care about unit cost. "€0.40 per 1,000 invoices at 97% accuracy" is a great line.

12
Flex

MCP server so it plugs into any AI assistant

Exposes read-only tools (get_cash_position, find_duplicates, explain_variance) over the Model Context Protocol, so Claude or another assistant can query the books with the same provenance guarantees.

Very current in 2026 and cheap to add once the tools exist.

13
Value

Auto-written monthly finance pack

A one-click PDF with cash, burn, runway, budget vs actual with explained variances, top vendors, recovered money and open exceptions. Every number is clickable in the web version.

The thing a founder would actually forward to investors, so it makes the product feel real.

Deliberately left out of v1

Actually sending payments, posting to a real ERP, payroll, tax filing and becoming an approved e-invoicing platform (a Plateforme Agréée). Each one adds compliance burden without making the demo better. Keep everything read-only plus recommendations.

6. Data sources

Public datasets (for extraction evals)

  • SROIE: 1,000 scanned receipts (company, date, address, total)
  • CORD: about 1,000 public receipts with line-level labels (CC BY 4.0)
  • FATURA: generated invoice layouts (good for layout variety)
  • Your own: 50–100 French invoices you render as Factur-X and as "ugly" scans

Note: SROIE and CORD are receipts, not B2B invoices. Say so in the eval report.

Synthetic company (for everything else)

  • Vendors with SIREN/VAT numbers, IBANs and addresses (all fake)
  • Invoices rendered to Factur-X PDF, UBL, and scanned PDF with noise
  • Bank statements as CSV and CAMT.053
  • Emails, including planted "bank details changed" attempts
  • An answer key listing every planted issue

7. Six-week build plan

About 15–20 hours a week. Each week ends with something you can demo.

Week 1

Foundations and the simulator

  • Ledger schema in Postgres: vendors, invoices, lines, transactions, matches, decisions (append-only)
  • Synthetic company generator with planted issues and an answer key
  • Render invoices to Factur-X, UBL and noisy scans; produce bank CSVs
Done when: one command generates a full fake company with a 12-month history.
Week 2

Ingestion, extraction and the first eval

  • Factur-X / UBL parser (exact path); vision-LLM extraction with per-field confidence (fallback path)
  • Model routing: XML first, then a small model, then a frontier model if confidence is low
  • Eval harness: field and line-item accuracy on SROIE, CORD, FATURA and your own set, plus cost per document
Done when: the first eval table is in the README, with accuracy and €/1k documents for each route.
Week 3

The money finders

  • Vendor entity resolution (Splink), with a review UI for merges
  • Fuzzy duplicate hunter: normalisation, candidate pairs, LightGBM pair scorer
  • Bank-change fraud guard: IBAN history, new-vendor velocity, email risk signals
Done when: the planted duplicates and fraud are found, with precision and recall reported, and the "€X recoverable" figure appears.
Week 4

Reconciliation and forecasting

  • Reconciliation, rules then ML then LLM: one-to-one, one-to-many, partial payments; an exception queue
  • 13-week cash forecast with conformal intervals; runway
  • Backtest against simple baselines and report coverage of the 80% band
Done when: the auto-match rate is shown and the forecast beats the baselines in the backtest.
Week 5

Agent, provenance and governance

  • Agent orchestrator using tools only; provenance IDs on every number; a validator that blocks unproven numbers
  • policy.yaml engine, approval queue, and an audit log with model version, inputs, confidence and approver
  • Plain-language scenarios mapped to structured forecast changes
  • 500-question finance eval set with answers taken from the answer key; MCP server
Done when: the eval shows task success, and unverified numbers equal 0.
Week 6

Polish, deploy and launch

  • Next.js dashboard: overview, duplicates, fraud holds, reconciliation, forecast, chat, approvals
  • Auto-generated monthly finance pack (PDF plus a clickable web version)
  • Docker, public demo running on the synthetic company, evals running in CI
  • README with the architecture diagram, eval tables and a 90-second demo video
Done when: there's a live link, the repo is public, and the launch post is scheduled.
